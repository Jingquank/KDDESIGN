# design-morph checks every frame, measures text in the browser, and gets a second eye

Stageshow, a skill for faux product UI, verifies its scenes with three things design-morph lacked: a recorder that flags any element changing far more in one frame than in the frames beside it, browser checks that measure text against its box, and a reviewer who sees only the frames, never the builder's reasons. Replaying the first film's published Library act through the first of these found what the 200-sample checker and the visual pass had both missed: a balance icon crossed the stage in one frame at 7.48 s, 1005 px, because the engine gave an element whose waypoint sat in its own group no layout window at all. So `check.mjs` now steps every act at 60 fps, the MP4's rate, and reports **snap** (a one-frame jump or pop) and **drift** (a card and its label, or a group sharing one `at`, pulling apart in flight); the engine gives such a waypoint its own slot; `tools/frames.mjs` has `texts()`, which seeks through the built film and reports any string wider than its own box, the case lesson 6 described and nothing checked; and step 9 ends with a reviewer started from REVIEW.md, who gets the sheets, the lessons and the list of exemptions and nothing else.

Two engine defaults changed with them. Colors mix in OKLab, and in OKLCH when both ends have a hue, so an accent turning into another accent keeps its chroma. Stagger is real time, 60 ms between items in reading order, tightening to 40 before a group counts as too long for its window, where it was a share of the window that ran from 13 ms to over 150 ms depending on the window and the count. And `scene.css` may no longer hold `@keyframes`, `animation` or `transition`: the browser's wall clock drives them, which a seek cannot reach, so stills and MP4 frames caught them at random.

On the Library film the new rules found seven problems (the balance icon, a count clipping it, an exit popping in 0.11 s, three groups too crowded for their stagger, three strings clipped mid-swap), all fixed in its data, and the reviewer then returned nine items the checks cannot see: a muddy middle state, chip labels with no destination, the account row crossing a tile. Not chosen: a stricter geometric checker for those, since they are judgments about story and color; the reviewer is the check for them, and a gold sheet joins it once a film passes. Not taken from Stageshow: its React kit and scripted scenes (ADR 0018 keeps runs to data), its framing rules (a morph's stage is the product's own viewport), and placeholder bars for unfocused text (the content question already curates what shows).
