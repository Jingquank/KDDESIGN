# A design-morph run on real products stays outside the repo; what it teaches comes back as fictional samples

The second worked example morphed one real product's web player into another's: two companies' pages, their logos and icons, album art, and the user's own account. It passed `check.mjs`, the text check and three review rounds, and then its owner saw at a glance what all of them had passed: in the middle state the player was a bar with a row of huge buttons. Each 16 px icon had taken its new 40 px box during layout while its art was still the old one, so the old glyph swelled to a size neither product uses. The reviewer had graded it C, finish, and the review ended by rule with it open.

The film itself cannot be a sample here: its stills show other companies' interfaces and a person's account, so they do not go into `gold/`, `evals/` or anywhere else in the repo, and the run's folder was deleted once the film was published as a private Artifact and rendered to MP4. What comes back is what it taught, rebuilt on fictional content: an **icon** rule in `check.mjs` (an old mask icon drawn over 1.5 times its starting size before its art swaps) with `evals/broken/icon-grows/`, lessons 16 (an icon keeps its glyph size) and 17 (a dark new page stays dark, after `oldSkin.auto` flipped a dark album page light in the middle state), and REVIEW.md grading a control at a size or shape neither product uses as B, readability, not C. Not chosen: keeping the real film as a private regression case outside the repo, which no one else could run, and a gold sheet of it, which would put the other products' UIs in the repo.
