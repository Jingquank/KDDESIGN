# crazy-prototype shapes the page to its Ideas

crazy-prototype laid every Idea out in one long scroll, each in its own section. When the Ideas were different forms of one prototype (one component built several ways, one screen with different layouts), the page repeated the same prototype again and again, and the user had to scroll between near-copies to compare what actually differed.

So the agent now decides the page's shape from how many Ideas there are and what they are. The skill names good practices, not steps: Ideas that are forms of one prototype often read best on a Stage, the prototype shown once at its real size with switches that move it between them; Ideas that stand apart usually want room of their own. The agent may follow them, mix them, or do something else. The only fixed part stays the same: every Idea keeps its number and tick, so the Pick sheet works as before.

Not chosen: making the Stage a required step or tying it to a count, since how to show the Ideas is a judgement about the Ideas, and crazy-prototype leaves layout to the agent.
