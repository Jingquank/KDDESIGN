# design-morph's review ends by rule, and what it keeps finding becomes a checker rule

The first three-act film went through five fresh-eye reviews, and every one returned a fresh list: each reviewer opened 10 to 15 of 70 stills, so each round found what the last had not looked at, and the brief ranked a tag over a label beside a story that could not be followed, so no list was ever empty. A review that can only end on "nothing" does not end. So the reviewer now grades every item A (story and paths), B (readability) or C (finish); the first review covers every frame; the same reviewer does the re-reviews, by SendMessage, and answers only whether its items are resolved and whether the touched stretches show a new A or B item. The film ships at the first round with no A and no B item, or after the third round, and everything still open goes to the hand-over by name.

Across those five rounds four kinds came back again and again: tags over text (all five rounds), the parts of a sidebar row coming apart (four), labels landing with no card (four), and an empty canvas between the old content leaving and the new arriving (three). Each is geometry the engine already knows, so `check.mjs` now reports them as **tag**, **drift** for row neighbours and for entering and leaving pairs, **orphan** and **void**, each with an eval. Run against the shipped film they found 17 problems, matching what the reviewers had kept naming. The reviewer is left with what geometry cannot see: whether the story reads, and whether the color does. Not chosen: a looser brief, which would stop the rounds by not looking, and an unlimited loop, which costs more each round and stops only by luck.
