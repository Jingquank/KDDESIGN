# The fresh-eye review

The brief for the reviewer started in step 9. The run gives the reviewer this file, the film's
contact sheets (`<film>/frames/sheet-<act>.png` and any full-size stills it shot), the gold sheets
in [gold/](gold/), [LESSONS.md](LESSONS.md), and the list of every `ok`, `clipOk`, `snapOk` and
`driftOk` in the scene with the ids it names. Nothing else: not the generator, not the checklist's
reasons, not the conversation, not the run's plan. The reviewer judges the picture, not the intent.

## The reviewer's brief

You are reviewing a Morph film someone else built: one product's old page turning into its new
page, element by element, skeleton first (grid and layout, then content, then the skin), with
guide lines and spec tags in the new product's accent. You have contact sheets of stills every
0.6 s, the gold sheets of a film that passed, the lessons the film is meant to follow, and the
list of exemptions the builder granted itself.

Look at the sheets the way the most exacting motion designer you know would: the one who
screenshots the single wrong frame and sends it back with "what is this?". Then answer, from a
frame each time:

1. **Story.** Can you tell, from the sheet alone, what changed and in what order? Does each stretch
   finish before the next one needs its space?
2. **Middle state.** In the frames where the new layout wears the old skin, is every string
   readable against its ground? Is any color passing through grey or mud?
3. **Paths.** Does anything fly through something else, leave a ghost behind, land in a place
   where it means something else, or pop in or out?
4. **Text.** Is any string clipped, overlapping another, or wrapping where it should not?
5. **Guides and tags.** Does a guide or tag cover what it explains, or stay on screen after its
   element has landed?
6. **Beside the gold.** Put each sheet next to the gold sheet: say the one difference. Is this film
   emptier, more crowded, muddier or harder to follow than the gold, and in which frames?
7. **Exemptions.** For every `ok`, `clipOk`, `snapOk` and `driftOk` on the list, find the frames
   where it applies and judge it again: does the frame read as designed, or as a collision that
   was silenced?

Return a ranked list, story and middle state first, then paths, then text, then guides and tags.
Each item: the sheet file and the time printed under the frame, what you would screenshot, and
the fix in one line. Say what you could not judge from stills (a pop between two frames, motion
speed). If there is nothing, say "nothing" and list the sheets you looked at.

Rules of the review:
- The bar for a finding is a frame. Quote the sheet and the time.
- Rank by how much of the film it spoils, not by how easy it is to fix.
- An exemption the builder granted is not settled: judge the frame again.
- Do not fix anything. Do not soften a finding because the film is nearly done.
- The products' own conventions are not faults: the old product's dark skin, the new product's
  type, colors and radii are what the film is about.
